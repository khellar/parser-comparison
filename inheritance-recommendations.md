# What to inherit from parsing and post-parsing

**Evidence reviewed: 16 November 2026. Status: proposals, not adopted changes.**

The best opportunity is to combine Template Parser's explicit compilation discipline with Parser Clerk's contextual interpretation and accountable admission. Several principles already exist in the measured ingress manual; strengthen the failing examples rather than adding another repetitive layer.

## Priority scorecard

| Priority | Recommendation | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| First | Preserve attitude and scope instead of substituting a weaker P | Directly observed semantic error with downstream consequences |
| First | Verify question-to-query-to-answer closure | Earlier study identifies a substantial fidelity gap |
| First | Separate linguistic commitment from evidential standing | A correct reading of a false claim must not become justified belief |
| First | Expose important uncovered meaning | Silent omission conceals the failure from the agent |
| Next | Localize reference uncertainty | Preserve usable event information with revisable identity |
| Next | Preserve history and explicit revision effects | Later clarification must change the current view without rewriting evidence |
| Next | Share examples and contract checks where useful | Reduce producer/consumer drift |
| Conditional | Normalize reasoning views and stage closure | Only after proving an engine/search problem |

Priorities are engineering recommendations derived from the evidence, not implementation authorization.

## 1. Preserve the operation applied to a proposition

**Observed:** In "I shall assume my conscience is clear", Template Parser preserved a future assumption with sealed content. Parser Clerk admitted a weaker clear-conscience claim and lost the assumption. A separate depiction example showed scope leakage in Template Parser, so this is a shared concern.

**Reason:** A weak assertion of P differs from an assertion that a person assumes, believes, quotes, depicts, requests, or asks about P.

**Candidate manual guidance:**

> Preserve the act or attitude the words express and the content it takes. Do not replace believing, assuming, quoting, depicting, requesting, or asking about P with a weaker standalone assertion of P. Preserve any separate commitment the speaker actually makes, with its own attribution and scope.

**Reuse:** First inspect existing reporting, belief, context, and event forms. This guidance is not a new grammar.

**Observable acceptance:** A matched set of assertion, belief, assumption, quotation, depiction, request, and question must retain the relevant act and embedded content. Embedded P must not independently support an unqualified P query. Separately asserted P must remain available. Negating an attitude must not accidentally affirm or deny its content.

## 2. Preserve question background at the correct scope

**Observed:** Template Parser kept Still inside the query for "Are you still coming?" Parser Clerk omitted the continuity. In the scarf choice question, Parser Clerk promoted branch-specific earlier possession to an unconditional claim.

**Counterevidence:** Template Parser sometimes kept genuinely shared temporal background inside a query without distinguishing its assumed status. Moving all background inside queries is not a general repair.

**Reason:** A question can convey background without asserting its answer. An alternative may license background that another alternative does not.

**Candidate manual guidance:**

> Preserve continuity, recurrence, and other background conveyed by a question. Keep background attached to the reading or alternative that licenses it. Do not turn the proposition being asked into an assertion, and do not promote branch-specific background into unconditional evidence.

**Observable acceptance:** Minimal pairs with still, again, already, and a choice question must preserve the distinction. Include an explicit challenge to the apparent presupposition. No confirmed answer may be invented, and not every background item needs a separate durable fact.

## 3. Localize uncertainty about references

**Observed:** Template Parser retained a separate uncertain pronoun-to-Jules Coref link; Parser Clerk directly assigned Jules as the actor.

**Reason:** An event may be clear while one participant is uncertain. A hidden firm identity can join unrelated histories; weakening the entire event can discard useful information.

**Candidate manual guidance:**

> When the event is clear but a reference is uncertain, preserve the uncertainty at the reference or interpretation that causes it. Use the existing candidate and rival mechanisms. Do not silently turn the favored candidate into source-confirmed identity.

**Reuse:** The measured manual already provides candidate identities and rival interpretations. Improve reliable use before inventing a second identity layer.

**Observable acceptance:** Test clear, ambiguous, favored-but-uncertain, same-name/different-individual, and later-corrected references. The later decision must update the interpretation without changing the source words or creating independent support.

**Boundary:** Do not transplant Template Parser's numerical "certain" threshold into durable Clerk policy.

## 4. Expose important semantic gaps

**Observed:** Template Parser provides unmapped spans and findings. Parser Clerk silently dropped new in "my new puppy". Template Parser also declared ordinary conversational content uncovered; transparency does not turn a gap into a success.

**Reason:** The agent needs to know what interpretation it could not preserve before choosing to clarify, retry, or rely on the result.

**Candidate manual guidance:**

> Preserve every distinction that could change identity, scope, time, quantity, commitment, or an appropriate response. When the available representation cannot preserve such a distinction, identify the affected words and unresolved meaning rather than silently dropping it.

**Reuse:** The existing add-nothing/drop-nothing rule is the starting point. Add examples and explicit outcomes rather than repeating the principle.

**Observable acceptance:** Test an identity-changing modifier, a quantity-changing modifier, an entailment-changing scope word, redundant wording, and an unsupported construction. Retain or disclose meaningful differences without making an atom for every word.

## 5. Inherit positive, boundary, and abstention examples

**Observed:** Template Parser catalogs combine construction descriptions, slots, examples, boundary cases, and explicit uncovered cases. Instructions and emission derive from structured material.

**Reason:** A positive example can encourage an overly broad rule. A close boundary example teaches the distinction that prevents the error.

**Proposal:** For each observed failure family, keep the intended distinction, a positive case, a close negative control, an ambiguity case, and the complete expected producer-to-consumer behavior.

Examples include request-shaped questions versus genuine ability questions, knowing a person versus knowing that P, depiction versus actual event, and a named source versus an impersonal report.

**Observable acceptance:** The boundary case must reject the incorrect generalization. A positive-only test is insufficient.

**Boundary:** Do not make a construction catalog a closed semantic ontology that prevents contextual interpretation or new predicates.

## 6. Close the question-to-query-to-answer path

**Observed:** In the older incomplete study, Parser Clerk produced 177 raw readable answers but 91 passed the intended-query check. Template Parser produced 109 raw readable answers and 101 passed. These counts do not prove every excluded answer was wrong; they show the answer was not justified as answering the intended question.

**Reason:** An engine can solve the wrong query correctly. A matching answer string is insufficient evidence.

**Proposal:** Reuse the question and reasoning contracts and make the following inspectable together:

- Original question.
- Interpreted question, including scope and entities.
- Formal query and variable roles.
- Premise references.
- Proof, explicit no-proof result, or engine failure.
- Rendered answer.

Distinguish question misunderstanding, query construction, retrieval, inference, proof reading, and answer expression.

**Observable acceptance:** Ask who delivered the parcels, how many were delivered, whether every parcel contained a book, an unsupported question, a paraphrase, and a swapped-role control. A familiar name returned to the wrong question must not count as success.

**Boundary:** This is a cross-seam requirement. A manual change alone cannot prove it.

## 7. Make answers readable without discarding proofs

**Observed:** Template Parser groups answers by referent while preserving underlying proofs and truth values.

**Reason:** People need coherent answers; the agent and reviewer need the derivations and dependencies.

**Proposal:** Group equivalent bindings in presentation, retaining every source-bound proof. Repeated derivations from one source must not look like independent confirmation.

**Observable acceptance:** One answer with several derivations renders once, exposes all proofs, preserves dependencies, does not multiply evidential force, and leaves incompatible answers visible as rivals.

## 8. Keep raw records and derive normalized reasoning views

**Observed:** Template Parser normalizes certain identities for reasoning while leaving original emitted records unchanged. This addresses search expansion and missing proofs on its engine path.

**Reason:** Reasoning may benefit from canonical names and deduplication while evidence review needs the original distinctions.

**Proposal:** Audit existing canonical identity and projection facilities first. A derived view should identify source records and version, identity decisions, substitutions, deduplication, and staleness conditions.

**Observable acceptance:** Correcting an identity produces a new view and identifies conclusions that depended on the old merge. Original records remain recoverable.

**Boundary:** A search optimization is not an authority to settle durable identity. Do not copy a numerical merge threshold without the destination's admitted identity semantics.

## 9. Declare downstream consumer capabilities

**Observed:** Template Parser separates emission from consumer-specific lowering and explicitly lists rules omitted for particular targets. The author's engine path and upstream engine target differ.

**Reason:** A correct stored structure may remain opaque or unusable to a reasoner. Storage is not execution.

**Proposal:** For important forms, state whether each consumer can store, match, substitute variables, infer, preserve provenance/truth semantics, and revise dependents.

**Observable acceptance:** An unsupported form yields an explicit limitation. It must not silently disappear, flatten into a different meaning, or count as success merely because it loaded.

**Boundary:** Attribute a limitation to the exact adapter and engine version where demonstrated.

## 10. Use staged closure only after diagnosing the problem

**Observed:** Template Parser uses a resolved view and staged kinship closure as documented workarounds for deeper-proof loss, search expansion, and repeated confidence effects around cycles.

**Reason:** The transferable contribution is diagnosis plus an explicit workaround. The exact workaround may be unnecessary or harmful in another engine.

**Observable acceptance before adoption:**

- Show a case the current path misses and the candidate recovers.
- Prevent double-counting of stored and rederived support.
- Preserve shared evidence dependencies.
- Update correctly after a premise changes.
- Bound execution and report an incomplete result when the bound is reached.

**Boundary:** Do not introduce a second reasoning authority or engine-specific unrolling solely because it exists in another pipeline.

## 11. Preserve immutable history and explicit revision effects

**Observed:** Template Parser records sentence availability, rejects future references, and signals revision-needed. Its inspected implementation explicitly leaves withdrawal bookkeeping unfinished. Parser Clerk encoded some corrections, but that does not prove every descendant updates.

**Reason:** The original reading must remain inspectable, while current standing reflects later clarification.

**Proposal:** Use addressable correction links and replayable changes to the current view. Availability metadata and a revision warning do not substitute for epistemic revision.

**Observable acceptance:** An early interpretation supports a conclusion, then a clarification changes the reference or premise. Preserve the original source, change the current view, identify dependent conclusions, preserve independent support, and reproduce the transition on replay.

## 12. Keep the sensor boundary at admission

**Observed:** Template Parser documents its STVs as sentence commitment. Fixed defaults are not independently calibrated world-truth estimates. Parser Clerk separates source claims, model proposals, parsed strength, and assessed evidence.

**Reason:** A perfect reading of a false statement is a successful parse, not a successful observation of the world.

**Proposal:** If Template Parser output enters Clerk, retain linguistic commitment as parse metadata and assess evidential contribution through existing source-aware admission.

Example: "I am absolutely certain the bridge is safe" supplies strong source commitment. Held bridge safety also depends on relevant source reliability, independent observations, contrary evidence, and later checks. A changed source assessment does not change the original words. Discovering an unsafe bridge does not itself prove that the earlier parse was wrong.

**Observable acceptance:** Hold words fixed while changing assessed source standing: the parse remains stable and evidential contribution changes through an attributable update. Then change words with the source fixed: parsed commitment changes without silently rewriting source reliability.

## 13. Do not import fixed STVs as epistemic policy

**Observed:** Fixed values such as [0.9, 0.7] for a weak hedge and [1.0, 0.99] for an individual fact are compiler conventions.

**Reason:** Source commitment, transcription fidelity, confidence that a report occurred, and belief that P is true are distinct. A shared numerical range does not imply a shared contract.

**Observable acceptance:** Every value crossing an integration boundary has a named interpretation and owner. No adapter equates quantities solely because both are called strength or confidence.

## 14. Start with obvious paired probes

| Probe | Distinction |
|---|---|
| Assertion / belief / assumption | Structural attitude versus weak P |
| Direct / quoted / depicted claim | Scope leakage |
| Plain question / still question | Background without confirmed answer |
| Choice question | Branch-specific background |
| Clear / ambiguous pronoun | Hidden identity certainty |
| Same name, different kind | Accidental merging |
| Factive wording from a doubtful source | Linguistic presupposition versus world belief |
| Fixed words, changed source assessment | Accountability |
| Reprints of one report | Duplicate versus independent support |
| Correction of a supporting premise | Descendant revaluation |
| Requested / completed event | Directive leakage |
| Intended / swapped-role question | Query fidelity |
| Supported / unsupported query | Explicit uncertainty versus silence |

These are proposed probes, not completed trials. Write the expected distinction before collection. Preserve inputs and host bindings, raw parse, checked/admitted representation, query/proof where relevant, uncovered outcomes, and a readable explanation.

## 15. Recommended sequence

1. Confirm the current manual and consumer contracts.
2. Add discriminating examples using existing forms.
3. Run the obvious probes and identify the failing seam.
4. Make the smallest repair that actually addresses that seam.
5. Verify producer-to-consumer closure, negative controls, corrections, and duplicate evidence.
6. Broaden evaluation with fresh material after the obvious cases work.

Keep the historical evaluation intact. The desired combined behavior is broad language understanding, inspectable interpretation, preserved uncertainty and provenance, faithful questions, and evidential force granted through accountable admission.
